5 Signs Your Washing Machine Needs a Repair

Quick Answer

Five signs a washing machine needs attention: longer cycles, water left behind, new noises, leaks and clothes that come out wetter or smellier than before. Catching them early usually costs less.

By the Ahmad Automatic Washing Repair team · Updated October 2026

Washing machines rarely die without warning. They get a little slower, a little noisier, a little less convincing, and we keep using them until the day they stop. Noticing the small changes is the cheapest repair there is.

Here are the five signs worth paying attention to.

Five Signs

1. Cycles are getting longer

A cycle that used to take an hour and now takes much longer can mean the machine is struggling to fill, drain or heat.

2. Water is left behind

A little water in the drum after a cycle, or in the door seal, is normal. A pool of it is not. See the drain guide.

3. New noises

Banging, grinding or squealing that was not there before. Loud noise during the spin often points at bearings, shock absorbers or an unbalanced load. See loud noise and vibration.

4. Leaks or damp patches

Even a slow drip is worth tracking down. See where leaks come from.

5. Clothes come out wetter, or smell

Damp clothes mean the spin is weak. A musty smell that survives cleaning can mean a drain problem or something trapped.

Repair or Replace?

If the machine is under about eight years old and one part is failing, repair is usually sensible. If it is much older, or repairs keep arriving for unrelated faults, replacement deserves a look. Our guide on what repairs cost covers the thinking.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if my washing machine needs repair?

Look for longer cycles, leftover water, new noises, leaks and clothes that come out wetter than before.

Is a noisy washing machine always broken?

No. Unbalanced loads and unlevel machines cause plenty of noise. Grinding that comes with every spin is more worrying.

When is replacement better than repair?

When the machine is old, the repair price is close to a new one, or repairs keep arriving for unrelated faults.
