Washing Machine Not Spinning: Causes and Fixes

Quick Answer

A washing machine that will not spin is often an unbalanced load, a drain problem or a door that is not latched. If those are fine, a worn belt, motor brushes or the control board may be to blame.

By the Ahmad Automatic Washing Repair team · Updated October 2026

The cycle ends, and the clothes come out dripping. The drum may turn by hand, but it will not spin when it matters. Before you assume the motor has died, there are a few cheap things to rule out.

Washers are built to refuse to spin when something looks wrong. That is annoying, but it usually means the machine is protecting itself, and the cause is simpler than you fear.

Check These First

  1. Open the door and spread the clothes out. A lump of wet towels or one blanket is the most common reason a spin stops, and many machines will retry or show a balance code.
  2. Check that the machine is not overloaded, or loaded with only one heavy item.
  3. Look for water left in the drum. Many machines will not spin until it drains. See what to check when it will not drain.
  4. Make sure the door is fully closed and the latch clicks.
  5. Check that the machine is level and does not rock when you push a corner.

If the Simple Things Are Fine

A worn belt

On belt-driven machines, a stretched or slipped belt gives a weak or absent spin. Many newer machines have a direct drive motor and no belt.

Worn carbon brushes

On brushed motors these wear down over time. Slow spinning, a burning smell or sparking sounds are hints.

Motor or control board

If the drum has no power at all, or the machine resets, the motor or board may need testing.

Lid switch on top loaders

If the machine does not think the lid is closed, it will not spin.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is my washing machine not spinning?

Common causes are an unbalanced load, a drain problem, a door that is not latched, a worn belt or motor brushes. A technician can check which.

Can an unbalanced load really stop the spin?

Yes. Most machines detect it and refuse to spin at speed. Spreading the clothes often fixes it.

Does my machine have a belt?

Many older and some current models do. Many newer front loaders use a direct drive motor with no belt.
