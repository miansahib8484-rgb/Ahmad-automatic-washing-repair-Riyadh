A dishwasher that stops cleaning is easy to blame and hard to judge from the sofa. Cloudy glasses, gritty plates, a puddle at the bottom after the cycle. It looks like the machine is finished. Very often it is not.

Dishwashers get blamed for a lot of things that turn out to be a clogged filter or a tired spray arm. We check the simple stuff first, then go further if we need to. Built-in or freestanding, the order is the same.

What Usually Goes Wrong

Dishes come out dirty or cloudy

Start with the filter at the bottom of the tub. Food collects there and starves the spray. Then the spray arms: if the little holes are blocked or the arm no longer spins freely, water never reaches the top rack. Hard water can leave a white film too. Our guide on a dishwasher that is not cleaning goes through the list, and the one on limescale applies to dishwashers as well.

Water stays at the bottom

A clogged filter, a kinked drain hose or a pump that has lost strength. See why a dishwasher is not draining.

Leaks

Usually the door gasket, a loose hose or a cracked spray arm. Too much detergent, or the wrong kind, can also foam over and look like a leak.

Dishes stay wet or the water stays cold

That points to the heating element or a temperature sensor.

Will not start, or stops with a code

A door latch that does not register, a power issue or a control board fault. Telling us the code helps.

Small Habits That Help

Clean the filter every few weeks. Scrape plates instead of rinsing them under the tap. Use a rinse aid if your water is hard, and do not overload the lower rack so the spray arm can turn. None of this is glamorous, and all of it extends the life of the machine.
